  \title{Noise Removal versus Signal Loss in Network Filtering of Daily GNSS Time Series: Internal Working Report, Pacific Northwest, Validation Years}

  \author{H. O\u{g}uz Bolat}
  \ead{oguz.bolat@std.yildiz.edu.tr}
  \affiliation{organization={Department of Geomatics Engineering, Y{\i}ld{\i}z Technical University},
    city={Istanbul},
  country={T\"urkiye}}

  \begin{abstract}
    This report describes a signal-loss audit of network filters for daily GNSS position time series on the Pacific Northwest network and reports its results on the validation years. Daily IGS20 solutions of the Nevada Geodetic Laboratory for 472 stations between $42.5^\circ$ and $49.5^\circ$N are reduced to residuals that keep each station's seasonal cycle and its earthquake offsets. Thirty-five filter configurations are audited: target-inclusive filters (network stack, sub-regional probabilistic PCA, robust weighted median), distance-excluded filters (the mean of every station beyond a radius $R$), leave-one-out filters over nearest neighbours, and learned filters, from a learned kernel to space--time attention networks trained with and without planted transients, each learned filter with three training seeds. Each is scored on the noise it removes, on its leave-one-out error on fixed masks, on the fraction it keeps of 711 planted Gaussian transients 10--100~km wide and on the spurious signal it creates outside them, and on three real signals without ground truth: the seasonal cycle, one coseismic offset and 15 slow-slip episodes. On the validation years (2020--2021) at $R = 0$, the far stack removes 26.8\% of the short-period scatter, has a mask error of 0.302 and keeps 0.985 and 0.774 of 25- and 100-km transients. The learned kernel over 256 neighbours has the lowest mask error, 0.292, and keeps 0.78 and 0.30. The attention networks trained with planted transients reach 0.297--0.304 and keep 0.92--0.95 and 0.61--0.66; trained without them, 0.296--0.303, 0.81--0.87 and 0.37--0.47. Across seeds, the mask error of a learned filter varies by at most 0.004 and its fraction kept of 100-km transients by up to 0.15. Every filter keeps 0.06--0.21 of the seasonal cycle and 0.06--0.22 of the slow-slip displacement, including the far stack at 400~km, which keeps every planted transient; 33 of the 35 filters report the one real offset as larger than observed. The test years have not been scored.
  \end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ===========================================================================

GNSS stations record crustal deformation together with noise. Part of the noise is common to many stations of a network (common-mode error), and arises from errors in satellite orbits and clocks, in the realisation of the reference frame and from large-scale environmental loading \citep{wdowinski1997, dong2006, kreemer2021}. Common-mode error has been estimated by stacking \citep{wdowinski1997}, by principal or independent component analysis \citep{dong2006, gruszczynski2018, zhou2022}, and by weighted means and medians of neighbouring stations \citep{tianshen2016, kreemer2021}. The evaluation reported for these filters is most often the reduction in the root-mean-square (RMS) scatter of the filtered series \citep{tianshen2016, kreemer2021, zhou2022}. \citet{kreemer2021} note that the main reason their filter reduces scatter more than traditional stacking is that it absorbs local common-mode transients.

Learned filters have been evaluated in other ways. \citet{bachelot2025} predict each station from its eight nearest stations beyond 400~km, a distance chosen empirically in their study area as the shortest at which slow-slip offsets were no longer reduced by denoising, and they assess signal preservation on 280 slow-slip offsets measured on raw and denoised series. \citet{costantino2024} add synthetic slow-slip events to noise generated from real GNSS series and report the error of the recovered displacement against the signal-to-noise ratio. \citet{mastella2025} train a single-station method on synthetic series and evaluate the noise removed from real series against the residual of a trajectory-model fit \citep{bedfordbevis2018}. \citet{tianshen2016} estimated the common mode from stations beyond increasing distance thresholds and used the threshold at which the RMS changed most sharply as the approximate extent of a regional transient. \citet{gualandi2016} evaluated source separation on simulated networks with known sources in synthetic noise. In Cascadia, \citet{costantino2026} report that the scaling, b-value and stress-drop distribution of slow-slip events change with the detection threshold applied to slip inverted from denoised data, and the debate over a reported precursory acceleration of slip \citep{bletery2023, bletery2025} turned on whether a common-mode correction removes only noise.

The study described here measures signal loss directly. Transients of known extent, amplitude and duration are planted in the real residuals of the Pacific Northwest network; each filter is run on the residuals with and without them; and the difference between the two cleaned series gives the fraction of each transient that survives. The same runs give the noise each filter removes and the spurious signal it creates where nothing was planted. Each filter predicts a station from $N$ other stations, excluding every station within a radius $R$ of it; both $N$ (from 16 stations to the whole network) and $R$ (from 0 to 600~km) are experimental variables. The network lies above the Cascadia subduction zone, whose episodic slow slip moves the surface by a few millimetres over weeks along hundreds of kilometres of the margin \citep{michel2019}, the kind of wide, coherent signal that averaging filters are expected to absorb. Section~\ref{sec:literature} reviews the literature, Section~\ref{sec:data} describes the data and their processing, Section~\ref{sec:audit} the audit, Section~\ref{sec:filters} the filters (Table~\ref{tab:filters}), Section~\ref{sec:results} the validation-year results, and Section~\ref{sec:open} lists open questions.

\section{Literature review}
\label{sec:literature}
%% ===========================================================================

This section is condensed from the research proposal. Two works cited there are left out: a blog post, and a study the author has not read in full.

\subsection{Classical common-mode filtering}

Network filtering of GNSS data began with the Southern California Integrated GPS Network, where the common-mode error was estimated as the weighted mean of the residuals at the other stations and subtracted from each series \citep{wdowinski1997}. Principal component analysis and the Karhunen--Lo\`eve expansion extended this to common modes that vary in space \citep{dong2006}. Later work made principal component filtering applicable to networks whose stations start and stop at different times: a modified analysis that reproduces each series from its principal components without filling gaps \citep{shen2014}, probabilistic principal component analysis that treats missing days as latent values \citep{gruszczynski2018}, and a variational Bayesian version that also selects the number of components \citep{li2020vbpca}. Because the common mode varies in space, large networks have been divided into sub-regions; on 208 Chinese stations, adaptive sub-regional filtering reduced residual scatter by 20--29\%, against 15--18\% for a single global analysis \citep{liu2026}. Independent component analysis offers an alternative decomposition \citep{gualandi2016, zhou2022}.

\citet{tianshen2016} weighted each neighbouring station by its distance and by the correlation of its residuals with the target, chose the weights by a grid search that minimises the residual scatter after filtering, and outperformed principal component analysis by more than 13\% in scatter reduction on about 1,600 stations in western North America. They also stepped through minimum-distance thresholds to separate long-range from regional common modes and to estimate the extent of regional transients. The common-mode component imaging of \citet{kreemer2021} takes a correlation-weighted median over nearby stations, including the target itself when it qualifies, and reduced residual scatter by 39--50\% on about 2,500 stations in western Europe. Across this family, performance is reported as scatter reduction, reduced inter-station correlation, velocity uncertainty or the recovery of a simulated common mode.

\subsection{Distance-excluded and learned filters}

The graph neural network of \citet{bachelot2025} represents stations as nodes and connects each to its eight nearest stations beyond 400~km. During training, 30\% of the nodes are masked and the error is computed on the masked nodes only, so the network learns to predict a station from distant stations, and the prediction is subtracted as noise. They compared it with three stacks, including a stack of all stations beyond 400~km, which performed comparably and better on the network-average metric. The 400~km was chosen as the shortest distance at which 280 slow-slip offsets from 15 Cascadia events were no longer reduced by denoising.

\citet{costantino2024} train a network of graph recurrent layers and transformers to extract slow slip from synthetic examples, slip on the subduction interface added to noise synthesised from real Cascadia series by principal component decomposition and Fourier phase randomisation; validated by synthetic recovery and agreement with tremor, it was later used to invert fifteen years of denoised data \citep{costantino2026}. \citet{mastella2025} model high-frequency scatter at single stations, with noise defined as the residual of a trajectory decomposition \citep{bedfordbevis2018} and synthetic training noise from an adversarial network. \citet{teutschmann2026} pretrained a self-supervised foundation model on 17,651 NGL stations by masked latent prediction, station by station, and fine-tuned it for displacement forecasting and the localisation of seismic offsets.

Predicting a withheld sensor from its neighbours is established elsewhere: tilt and compliance noise is removed from ocean-bottom seismometers by predicting the vertical channel from other channels \citep{crawfordwebb2000, janiszewski2019}, distributed acoustic sensing records are denoised by predicting each channel from its neighbours \citep{batson2019, vandenende2023}, and graph networks for spatiotemporal kriging are trained by reconstructing withheld sensors \citep{wu2021ignnk, cini2022grin, marisca2022spin}. Excluding nearby observations so that spatial autocorrelation does not inflate apparent skill is the basis of buffered spatial cross-validation \citep{lerest2014}.

\subsection{How signal preservation has been assessed}

Three approaches recur (Table~\ref{tab:assessment}). Observed transients can be compared before and after filtering \citep{bachelot2025}; this uses real signals but has no ground truth. Synthetic signals can be added to synthetic noise \citep{costantino2024, mastella2025}; this has ground truth but depends on how realistic the noise is, and has been analysed against signal-to-noise ratio rather than spatial scale. Independent data such as tremor can confirm that an extracted signal is real \citep{costantino2024}, but not measure what was lost. Detectors of transients are commonly trained on synthetic signals: \citet{costantino2023} injected 60,000 synthetic slow-slip events computed with elastic dislocations \citep{okada1992} into surrogate noise and recovered 35 of 40 catalogued events \citep{michel2019}; other approaches include training on real labelled windows \citep{tanaka2025}, autoencoders for InSAR time series \citep{rouetleduc2021}, the network strain filter \citep{ohtani2010}, Kalman smoothing with principal components \citep{jiherring2013} and sparse estimation \citep{riel2014}.

\subsection{Gaps}

Four gaps motivate the study. No study has measured, with ground truth, how much of a transient a common-mode filter removes as a function of the transient's size relative to the filter's reach. The reach has been chosen empirically, as a fixed 400~km \citep{bachelot2025} or by scatter-based thresholds \citep{tianshen2016}. Filters that use the target station, which include most classical methods, have not been compared with leave-one-out filters on signal loss. And learned filters have been compared with simple ones on noise reduction, not at equal signal kept.

\subsection{Data}
\label{sec:stations}

The data are the daily IGS20 solutions of the Nevada Geodetic Laboratory (NGL) \citep{blewitt2018, ngl2026}. Each solution gives the east, north and up position of a station's antenna relative to a fixed reference point near the station, estimated from 24~hours of observations, with formal uncertainties of about 1~mm in the horizontal and 3--4~mm in the vertical. NGL publishes new days with a latency of about ten days.

All NGL stations between $42.5^\circ$ and $49.5^\circ$N and between $125.5^\circ$ and $116.0^\circ$W with at least 1,095 daily solutions were selected on 5 November 2026: 540 stations of the Pacific Northwest Geodetic Array, the Network of the Americas and other networks. Their median distance to the nearest station is 6.2~km. The cube built on 5 November 2026 keeps 472 of them: 5 had no baseline, 16 were removed by the station quality control of Section~\ref{sec:residuals} and 47 were co-located twins within 1~km of a station with more daily solutions. It covers 6,872 days, from 1 January 2008 to 24 October 2026, with 69\% of the station-days available (77\% in the validation years), and 432 stations have data in the validation years. A station has a median of 244 stations within the 300-km neighbour search at $R = 0$ and 132 at $R = 400$~km (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}).

\subsection{Residuals}
\label{sec:residuals}

For each station $i$ and component $c$, the trajectory model
\begin{equation}
  x_{i,c}(t) = a + v\,(t - t_0) + \sum_{k=1}^{2}\left[p_k \sin(2\pi k t) + q_k \cos(2\pi k t)\right] + \sum_{j} b_j\, H(t - t_j) + r_{i,c}(t)
  \label{eq:trajectory}
\end{equation}
is fitted by least squares on the training years (2008--2019), where $a$ is a constant, $v$ a linear rate about the mean epoch $t_0$ of the fitted data, $p_k$ and $q_k$ the amplitudes of the annual and semi-annual harmonics, $b_j$ the amplitudes of Heaviside steps $H$ at the epochs $t_j$ of the NGL step catalogue, $r_{i,c}(t)$ the residual and $t$ the time in years. A step is estimated only where at least 30 epochs lie on each side of it. A station with fewer than 365 daily solutions in the training years is fitted on the first two years of its record, or on its first 365 solutions if these reach further; such stations are always among the held-out ones (Section~\ref{sec:heldout}). The fitted trajectory is extrapolated to the later years.

The residual passed to the filters is the series minus the constant, the rate and the equipment steps. The seasonal terms are estimated so that an incomplete cycle does not bias the rate, and then added back, so the residual carries each station's seasonal cycle; what a filter does to it is measured directly (Section~\ref{sec:measures}). Earthquake steps are likewise estimated and added back, so that coseismic offsets remain in the series. Equipment steps are removed everywhere: within the training years they are estimated in the fit, and after them by a local estimate, the median residual over the 30 days after the change minus that over the 30 days before, with at least 5 days on each side.

A value more than five robust standard deviations (1.4826 times the running median absolute deviation) from its 61-day running median in any component is treated as missing. For station screening only, a second fit is made from which the earthquake steps and the seasonal terms are also removed, so that real offsets and the seasonal cycle do not count as noise; a station is dropped if the robust scale of this fit exceeds 10~mm in the horizontal or 30~mm in the vertical, which removes non-tectonic site motion. The second fit changes no series.

Stations that develop problems after the training years are kept as inputs but not scored (scoring QC): those whose day-to-day scatter in the validation and test years exceeds three times their training-years scatter, and those with a jump of more than 50~mm in the horizontal or 100~mm in the vertical between consecutive 30-day medians with no catalogued step within 45 days. Eight stations are left out of scoring, all for unexplained jumps; the first jump falls in the validation years at two of them (CLHQ, January 2020; BLYN, August 2020) and in the test years at six (BCNN, MGRB, P688, IDFB, CPCO and SEPR, 2022--2025). The scoring QC reads each station's own series in the validation and test years and nothing else, and is the same for every filter.

The residuals, their formal uncertainties and an availability mask are stored in the cube, with the neighbour lists of every station at each exclusion radius. Days are split into training (2008--2019), validation (2020--2021) and test years (2022 onward). The local equipment-step estimate, the outlier screening and the scoring QC use days from every year; their effect on a day that is kept is at most a constant shift or the removal of a spike.

\subsection{Held-out stations and excluded intervals}
\label{sec:heldout}

The held-out stations are never used in training, and serve to test how well trained filters generalise to stations they have not seen. Ten percent of the stations are chosen by farthest-point sampling \citep{gonzalez1985} with seed 0, which starts from a random station and repeatedly adds the station farthest from all those already chosen; the stations with fewer than 365 training-year solutions are added, giving 77 held-out stations, 35 of them short-record stations. The trajectories of the short-record stations are fitted on the first part of their record, which can extend into the validation or test years.

The Cascadia slow-slip episodes of the training years are excluded from training, so that no filter is fitted or trained on them: the episodes of the GNSS catalogue of \citet{michel2019}, each over its along-strike extent from the paper's source data, bar 13.5\% of the available training station-days, at 251 stations. Learned filters are in addition trained only outside $\pm 30$ days of catalogued M $\ge$ 6 earthquakes at a station.

\subsection{Displacement rather than increments}

Filters are applied to displacement rather than to daily increments. For example, 2~cm of postseismic displacement accumulated over a year is a daily increment of about 0.055~mm, much smaller than the day-to-day scatter of the increments. Increments are used only as an additional input to the M5 attention network (Section~\ref{sec:filters-learned}).

\section{The signal-loss audit}
\label{sec:audit}
%% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Leave-one-out filtering and the exclusion radius}
\label{sec:loo}

A filter $P$ estimates the part of the residual of station $i$ that is shared with other stations, and the cleaned series is $r - P(r)$. A leave-one-out filter never reads station $i$ itself when estimating it, and never reads any station within $R$~km of it. The target-inclusive filters of Section~\ref{sec:filters-inclusive} read the target on purpose, as published filters of their kind do; they are applied to the same cube and scored on noise removed and on the signal measures, but not on the masks. The exclusion radius $R$ is an experimental variable. The far stack is run at $R = 0$, 25, 50, 100, 200, 400 and 600~km; the nearest-neighbour filters (M1 to M4) and the learned filters are trained and scored on the masks at the cube's radii of 0, 10, 25, 50, 100, 200 and 400~km, the whole-network M6 variants up to 100~km. The nearest-neighbour filters are run through every signal test at every radius; the learned filters at $R = 0$ and 25~km with seed 0 and at $R = 0$ with seeds 1 and 2.

Two leak checks are run for every leave-one-out filter at every radius, on the first year of the validation period. Both compare predictions on two inputs that differ only in values the filter must not read. In the radius check, the target and every station within $R$ of it are set to a poison value of $10^6$~mm, for five randomly chosen targets. In the mask check, the cells hidden for the mask scores are filled once with missing values and once with the poison value, while remaining marked as unavailable. In both, the predictions must agree to within 0.001~mm. All 183 recorded checks passed, the 118 training runs of the overnight queue of 6--7 November 2026 among them. Because a filter that passes the radius check never reads the target, one prediction on the unhidden cube is a leave-one-out prediction for every station at once, and this is how all cleaned series are produced.

\subsection{Measures}
\label{sec:measures}

Filters are compared on two measures of noise and five of signal, all computed on the validation years. The planted transients are the primary signal test; the real signals are secondary checks without ground truth.

Noise removed is the reduction in short-period scatter at each scored station,
\begin{equation}
  1 - \frac{\mathrm{rms}\big(\mathrm{fast}(r - P(r))\big)}{\mathrm{rms}\big(\mathrm{fast}(r)\big)},
  \label{eq:noise}
\end{equation}
where the fast part of a series is the series minus its 61-day running median. It is averaged over the three components and summarised by its median over the 395 scored stations. Predicting zero gives 0. The measure covers the fast band only; common-mode error at periods longer than about two months earns no credit here.

The mask error is the leave-one-out error on fixed evaluation masks (seed 20260101) of the validation days: 10\% of the available station-days of the seen stations at random (\texttt{scatter}), one 30-day block per seen station and year (\texttt{block}), and every day of the held-out stations (\texttt{station}). At each hidden cell the error $r - P(r)$ is taken, its fast part is formed, its root mean square is normalised by each station's robust noise scale from the training years, and the result is averaged over the three components and summarised by its median over stations. Days within $\pm 30$ days of an M $\ge$ 6 earthquake catalogued for a station are left out. Predicting zero gives 0.423 on \texttt{scatter} rather than 1, because separating the slow part also removes some of each station's own noise. The leave-one-out filters were tuned, and the learned filters early-stopped, on a related criterion, the same fast error over every available validation day of the seen, scored stations (the validation criterion of Table~\ref{tab:training}); the two are computed on different cells and are not equal. The error is lower when the prediction reproduces whatever the target shares with other stations, signal included, so tuning on it favours settings that absorb coherent signal; the signal measures below are independent checks on what the tuning selected.

Retention measures the fraction of a planted transient that survives filtering. A transient $s$ is added to the residuals, and both the original and the modified residuals are filtered. The change in the cleaned series and the retention are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta = \big(r + s - P(r+s)\big) - \big(r - P(r)\big),
  \qquad
  \rho = \frac{\langle \Delta,\, s \rangle}{\langle s,\, s \rangle},
  \label{eq:retention}
\end{equation}
with the inner products over the horizontal components, the days of the transient and the scored stations within $2L$ of its centre, where $L$ is its width. Pairing with the unmodified run removes the noise term; for a linear filter, $\rho = \langle s - P(s), s\rangle / \langle s, s \rangle$ exactly. $\rho = 1$ means that the transient is kept, $\rho = 0$ that it is removed, and $\rho > 1$ that the filter adds to it. Retention is summarised by its median over transients.

Spurious signal is the change created where nothing was planted. A Gaussian transient is set to zero beyond $3L$ from its centre (its footprint). At the scored stations outside the footprint whose cleaned series the transient can change (stations with a neighbour inside the footprint for the nearest-neighbour filters, stations whose far set includes one for the far stack, every station of the same region for C3, every station for C1 and for the learned filters that read the far stack or the whole network), the peak horizontal amplitude of $\Delta$ is taken as a fraction of the amplitude of the transient, the largest value over these stations is kept, and its median over transients is reported, with the median number of such stations.

Seasonal kept fits, per scored station and component over the validation years, a constant, a rate, annual and semi-annual terms and Heaviside steps at the station's catalogued earthquakes to $r$ and to $r - P(r)$ on the same days, and reports $\langle c_{\mathrm{kept}}, c_{\mathrm{obs}} \rangle / \langle c_{\mathrm{obs}}, c_{\mathrm{obs}} \rangle$ over the four harmonic coefficients $c$, per component (E, N, U), over the two horizontal components (H) and over all three. It is summarised by its median over the 372 stations scored. Unfiltered, the median annual amplitudes are 2.40, 3.75 and 6.14~mm in east, north and up, and the semi-annual 0.78, 0.91 and 0.86~mm.

Real offsets are every catalogued earthquake step in the scored period at a scored station with an observed horizontal offset of at least 3~mm and no other step within 15 days. The offset is the mean position over the 10 days after the earthquake minus that over the 10 days before (at least 5 available days on each side), and the fraction kept is $\langle \text{kept}, \text{observed} \rangle / \langle \text{observed}, \text{observed} \rangle$, with ``kept'' the same difference on the cleaned series. On the validation years there is one such offset: 4.89~mm at IDNR from the 31 March 2020 M6.5 central Idaho earthquake (catalogue identifier us70008jr5).

Slow-slip episodes are scored from a fixed list. For each episode, at the scored stations within its latitude range and within 150~km east or west of the tremor band axis, the observed horizontal displacement is the mean position over the 10 days after its end minus the mean over the 10 days before its start (at least 5 available days in each window), and the row's retention is the projection of the same quantity on the cleaned series, as for the offsets. The median over stations is taken per episode, and the median over the episodes with at least 10 scored stations is reported. A validation run may score an episode only if its whole measure window lies on training or validation days; the episodes of the training years qualify because they are excluded from training (Section~\ref{sec:heldout}). The validation run scores 18 episodes of the GNSS catalogue of \citet{michel2019}, of which 15 have at least 10 scored stations (1,701 station-episodes). The 31 tremor-timed episodes of 2022 onward lie in the test years and are not scored. These observed signals have no ground truth: the observed displacement includes noise and the seasonal cycle over the window.

\subsection{Planted transients}
\label{sec:planted}

Gaussian transients have the form
\begin{equation}
  s(d, t) = A \exp\!\left(-\frac{d^2}{2L^2}\right) g(t)\, \mathbf{u},
  \label{eq:gaussian}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the distance of a station from the centre, $A$ the amplitude, $L$ the width, $\mathbf{u}$ a horizontal unit vector in a random direction and $g(t)$ the time profile: a raised-cosine rise over $D$ days, a 60-day hold and a raised-cosine fall over $D$ days, after which the transient is zero, so it leaves no permanent offset. $s$ is zero beyond $3L$, and every station in the footprint moves in the same direction. The grid is $A \in \{2, 5, 10\}$~mm, $L \in \{10, 25, 50, 100\}$~km and $D \in \{10, 30, 90\}$~days. Each of the 36 combinations is planted at 20 centres, each at a randomly chosen scored station, giving 720 transients per filter and radius, of which 708--712 have at least one scored station within $2L$ (711 for the far stack at $R = 0$). The median number of scored stations within $2L$ is 3, 10, 38.5 and 125.5 for $L = 10$, 25, 50 and 100~km. Onsets fall in 300-day slots of the validation years, and the signal is added only on days with data. Many transients share one run of a filter: two share a run only if no station's cleaned series can be changed by both. The library of fault-slip events of the Californian development is not used on this network.

\subsection{Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

All design decisions, hyper-parameters and model choices are made on the validation years. The scoring code scores only the validation split unless the test split is requested explicitly, and no filter has been scored and no signal test run on the test years of this network; their days are read only by the screening steps of Section~\ref{sec:residuals} (the local equipment-step estimate, the outlier screening and the scoring QC). The method will be frozen, with a tagged version of the software, before the test years are scored once with the settings chosen on validation; until then nothing here is final. Each learned filter was trained with three seeds at $R = 0$. Differences between filters are to be tested with a paired block bootstrap over stations and 30-day blocks for the error, and by resampling the planted transients, paired by identifier, for retention; these tests have not yet been run on this network, so the only uncertainty reported here is the spread across training seeds.

\subsection{Target-inclusive filters}
\label{sec:filters-inclusive}

C1 is the stack of \citet{wdowinski1997}: the common mode of every station on a day is the unweighted mean of all available stations that day, the station itself included. C3 is sub-regional probabilistic PCA \citep{tipping1999}, applied as in \citet{gruszczynski2018} with regions as in \citet{liu2026}: stations are grouped into 1, 4 or 8 regions by k-means on their projected coordinates; in each region and component, a probabilistic PCA with 1, 2 or 3 components is fitted by expectation--maximisation on the quiet training residuals (Section~\ref{sec:filters-learned}), normalised per station, with missing days treated as latent; on any day the component scores are estimated from every available station of the region, the target included, and the common mode is the reconstruction. The nine settings are labelled c3-k$k$p$p$ for $k$ regions and $p$ components. M2-self is the robust weighted median of M2 (Section~\ref{sec:filters-loo}) with the target's own value added to the median, as in common-mode component imaging \citep{tianshen2016, kreemer2021}; it reuses M2's tuned settings.

\subsection{Distance-excluded filters}
\label{sec:filters-distance}

The far stack is the unweighted mean of every available station at least $R$ from the target, the target excluded, per day and component. It has no parameters. D1 is the far stack at $R = 400$~km, the distance of \citet{bachelot2025}, computed separately; its results agree with those of the far stack at 400~km. \citet{bachelot2025} use the eight nearest stations beyond 400~km in a graph neural network and compare it with a stack of the same stations; D1 uses every station beyond 400~km.

\subsection{Leave-one-out filters over nearest neighbours}
\label{sec:filters-loo}

These filters predict the target from its $K$ nearest stations beyond $R$, using only day $t$ (M1--M3) or the neighbours' whole series (M4). Their settings were chosen on the validation years of this network by the validation criterion.

M1 is the weighted mean $\hat r_i(t) = \sum_j w_{ij} a_j(t) r_j(t) / \sum_j w_{ij} a_j(t)$ over the 16 nearest stations, with $w_{ij} = \exp(-d_{ij}^2/2\ell^2)/\bar\sigma_j^2$, $a_j(t)$ the availability and $\bar\sigma_j$ the station's median formal uncertainty over the training years, per component. The length scale $\ell$ was chosen from 10~km to infinity (a plain mean): 50~km for 16 neighbours and 100~km for 64 and 256. At $R = 0$ the validation criterion is 0.328 for 16 neighbours, 0.318 for 64 and 0.315 for 256 at the chosen $\ell$, and within 0.004 of these values for every $\ell \ge 50$~km.

M2 follows \citet{kreemer2021}: a neighbour's value on day $t$ is dropped if it differs from that neighbour's own 31-day running median by more than $k$ times its robust scale; the rest are combined by a weighted median with M1's weights, interpolated as in \citet{kreemer2021}. With fewer than three neighbours left it falls back to M1. The tuned values at $R = 0$ are $\ell = 100$~km and $k = 2$.

M3 is ridge regression on the three components of the 16 neighbours, an availability bit per neighbour and an intercept, in units of each station's noise scale. Missing neighbours are set to zero and the remaining values rescaled by $K$ divided by the number available, so that gaps do not bias the fit. M3a fits one weight vector per station and component ($\lambda = 0.1$ in units of the trace of the Gram matrix per feature); the 77 held-out stations use M3b. M3b fits one weight vector per component for all stations, adding each neighbour slot's log distance and azimuth and their products with the slot's residuals ($\lambda = 0.001$).

M4 applies M3 separately to four period bands that add up exactly to each series (under 7, 7--60, 60--400 and over 400 days), obtained with differences of zero-phase Gaussian smoothers, and sums the four predictions; $\lambda$ is chosen per band. Before filtering, each series' gaps are filled by linear interpolation of its own days, and a neighbour missing on a day contributes zero with its availability bit off. The band filters read the neighbours' past and future days. M4a is per station and M4b pooled, as M3a and M3b.

\subsection{Learned filters}
\label{sec:filters-learned}

The learned filters are trained on quiet residuals: the residuals minus the earthquake offsets estimated in the training-years fit, on training days outside the slow-slip exclusion and outside $\pm 30$ days of catalogued M $\ge$ 6 earthquakes, at stations that are not held out. The loss is the squared error in units of each station's robust noise scale. Training stops early on the validation criterion: the attention networks are evaluated every 500 steps and stop after 3,000 steps without improvement, M3k after six epochs without improvement. Every learned filter was trained with seed 0 at each of its radii and with seeds 1 and 2 at $R = 0$ (Table~\ref{tab:training}). The architectures and training settings are those of the Californian development, carried over unchanged.

M3k keeps M1's form and learns its kernel: $w_{ijc} = \mathrm{softplus}(f_\theta(g_{ij}, q_j))_c$, where $g_{ij}$ is the neighbour's log distance and the sine and cosine of its azimuth from the target, $q_j$ its log robust noise scale per component and record completeness, and $f_\theta$ a multilayer perceptron with two hidden layers of 32 units. The weights depend on where a neighbour is and what kind of station it is, never on its values, so once trained M3k is a fixed weighted mean of day $t$. It is trained over 16 and over 256 neighbours.

M5 is a space--time attention network. A sample is a target, its 32 nearest stations beyond $R$, chosen for each 64-day window among the 64 nearest by availability, and one summary node, the far stack. Each node has 17 input channels per day: residual, log formal uncertainty, availability, a flag for cells hidden by a training mask, the daily increment $r(t) - r(t-1)$ (zero unless both days exist), the station's log noise scale and record completeness, and the day of year (sine and cosine), all in units of the station's noise scale. A convolution cuts each node's window into 4-day tokens; three blocks then alternate attention over a node's own tokens (time) and attention over the nodes at the same token (space), the latter biased by each node's log distance, azimuth and type relative to the target ($d = 64$, four heads). The output is a correction added to the far stack, so training starts from the far stack. The target is hidden for the whole window, so predictions never read it. Prediction uses 64-day windows overlapping by half, combined with a triangular taper, so a day's prediction reads neighbours' days before and after it.

M6 gives the network a wider view than M5's 32 neighbours and one summary node. Its inputs are the residuals alone, per station and day in units of the station's noise scale, and an availability bit; geometry enters only as a learned attention bias for each pair of stations, from their log distance and azimuth, so no absolute coordinates are seen. A convolution cuts each station's 64-day window into 4-day tokens, and three blocks alternate attention over a station's own tokens (time) and attention over all stations of the sample at the same token (space), with $d = 64$ and four heads. In the whole-network versions (m6, m6r) a sample is every station: a random share of 5--30\% of the stations is hidden as targets, together with every station within $R$ of a target, and the targets are predicted from the rest. In the $N$-nearest versions (m6r16, m6r64, m6r256) a sample is one target and its $N$ nearest stations beyond $R$. In the far-stack residual variants (the \texttt{r} in the name) the output is a correction added to the far stack and the target's own input slot carries its far-stack series, as in M5.

The -aug versions are trained with planted fields. In half of the training samples a synthetic field is added to the inputs of every node, and to the far-stack series as it would enter that mean, but not to the targets, so copying it into a prediction increases the loss. For M5-aug the field is one Gaussian blob or dipole centred within $R + 300$~km of the target, 5--150~km wide, of 1--15 times the station's noise scale, lasting 5--60 days with a raised-cosine rise and fall, in a random horizontal direction; the M6 -aug versions add one to three such fields. The generator uses its own seed and shapes and runs on training days only, so it is separate from the evaluation transients.

\subsection{Seasonal kept}
\label{sec:res-seasonal}

Every filter removes most of the seasonal cycle (Table~\ref{tab:results-real}). At $R = 0$, seasonal kept over all components lies between 0.06 (M5) and 0.21 (M3a); the far stack and C1 keep 0.125. Over the stations, the far stack's value has a median of 0.125 and 10th and 90th percentiles of $-0.11$ and 0.38. The ridge regressions keep the most (M3a 0.210, M3b 0.191, M4a 0.187, M4b 0.189); M1 and M2 keep 0.095 and 0.083. The far stack keeps 0.125--0.137 from $R = 0$ to 200~km, 0.169 at 400~km and 0.355 at 600~km. For the learned filters, the seed spread of seasonal kept (up to 0.094) is comparable to the range of their seed means (0.062--0.132).

\subsection{Real earthquake offset}
\label{sec:res-offsets}

The validation years contain one offset that passes the selection: 4.89~mm at IDNR from the 31 March 2020 M6.5 central Idaho earthquake. The unfiltered series gives 1.00 by construction. Every filter except two C3 settings reports more than the observed offset: the far stack 1.461 (1.461--1.509 over its radii), C1 1.457, the filters over 16 neighbours 1.26--1.32 and the learned filters 1.30--1.52 (seed means 1.31--1.48). C3 with 4 regions and 3 components keeps 0.812, and with 8 regions and 3 components 0.404. With one offset at one station, this measure has no statistical weight on the validation years.

\subsection{Slow-slip episodes}
\label{sec:res-episodes}

The validation run scores 18 episodes of the GNSS catalogue of \citet{michel2019}; the 15 with at least 10 scored stations enter the summary (Table~\ref{tab:episodes}). Their observed displacements have medians of 2.2--8.0~mm per episode. The far stack keeps 0.183 (median over episodes), C1 the same, D1 at 400~km 0.200, and the far stack at 600~km 0.369. The learned filters keep 0.06--0.13 (seed means), and their seed spread reaches 0.074. Per episode, the far stack's value ranges from $-0.005$ to 0.981; it keeps 0.53 of M19-16\_18\_19 and 0.61 of M19-51, and $-0.005$ to 0.034 of M19-14\_15, M19-43\_44 and M19-47+50\_48.

\section{Open questions}
\label{sec:open}
%% ===========================================================================

These are questions the validation numbers raise, listed for the author's decision; none has been acted on.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Seasonal kept and episodes kept are low for every filter, including the far stack at 400~km, which keeps every planted transient (Sections~\ref{sec:res-seasonal} and~\ref{sec:res-episodes}). Whether this is the network-wide part of the seasonal cycle and of the episode windows, or an artefact of the measures, has not been checked on individual stations.
  \item The real-offset measure rests on one offset at one station on the validation years, and 33 of 35 filters report it as larger than observed (Section~\ref{sec:res-offsets}). Whether to keep it, widen its selection, or report it only on the test years is open.
  \item The slow-slip exclusion bars 13.5\% of the available training station-days (Table~\ref{tab:coverage}), and there is no catalogued GNSS episode list for 2017--2022 to score or exclude.
  \item The architectures and training settings of the learned filters were carried over from California. M3k stopped at its first or second epoch at $R = 0$ on every seed (Section~\ref{sec:res-training}).
  \item The scoring QC reads each station's own series in the test years, and six of the eight stations it leaves out were flagged by a jump first seen there (Section~\ref{sec:residuals}).
  \item The paired bootstrap comparisons of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} have not been run on this network, so differences between filters are judged here against the seed spread only.
\end{enumerate}
